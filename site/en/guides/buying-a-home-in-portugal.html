How to buy a home in Portugal as a foreigner

Buying in Portugal from abroad is simpler than most people expect. Foreigners can buy property on the same terms as Portuguese citizens, and you do not even need to be in the country to sign. Here is the whole process in six steps, the costs to plan for, and the mistakes I see most often.

By Liliana Pinto, licensed real estate consultant in Porto (Douro Lovers, AMI 18696). I work with international clients in English, French and Portuguese.

1 Get your NIF (Portuguese tax number)

The NIF (número de identificação fiscal) is your tax number in Portugal. You need it to buy a home, open a bank account, sign utility contracts and even to rent. It is the first thing to sort out.

  • You can get it in person at a tax office (Finanças) or remotely, through a lawyer or a tax representative.
  • If you live outside the European Union, you will usually need a tax representative in Portugal.
  • Remotely, it often takes a few days to a couple of weeks.

2 Open a Portuguese bank account

It is not strictly required by law, but in practice almost everyone needs one: to pay the deposit, the taxes and, later, the utilities and the yearly property tax (IMI). It is also essential if you want a Portuguese mortgage.

  • You will need your passport, your NIF, proof of address and proof of income.
  • Several banks let non-residents open an account remotely or through a lawyer with power of attorney.

3 Set your real budget

The asking price is not the final price. As a rule of thumb, add about 7 to 8% on top of the price for taxes and fees (see the cost breakdown below). If you need a mortgage, get a pre-approval before you start visiting: you will know your real budget, and sellers will take your offer more seriously.

  • Non-residents can usually borrow 60 to 70% of the value of the home.
  • Terms can go up to 40 years, depending on your age.
  • Banks ask for your passport, NIF, proof of income, bank statements and credit history.

4 Find your home and make an offer

Tell your consultant what matters to you: area, size, budget, timeline and the life you want here. A good shortlist saves you trips. I visit homes with my clients on live video, so you can see the home properly before you travel.

Before you make an offer, make sure the paperwork is checked:

  • Caderneta predial: the tax record of the property.
  • Certidão do registo predial: the land registry certificate, which shows the owner and any mortgage or debt on the home.
  • Energy certificate, mandatory for any sale.
  • Licença de utilização: the habitation licence, or proof that the building does not need one.

Offers are usually made in writing. Price, deposit, deadlines and what is included (furniture, appliances, parking) are all negotiable.

5 Sign the promissory contract (CPCV) and pay the deposit

Once your offer is accepted, buyer and seller usually sign a CPCV (contrato-promessa de compra e venda). It fixes the price, the date of the deed and the conditions of the sale, and you pay a deposit, often 10 to 20% of the price.

  • If the buyer pulls out without a valid reason, they lose the deposit. If the seller pulls out, they usually have to pay back double.
  • A lawyer should read the CPCV before you sign. With a power of attorney, they can also sign it for you.

6 Sign the deed (escritura) and get your keys

On the day of the deed, you pay the rest of the price, IMT and stamp duty are paid just before, and the home is registered in your name. The deed is signed at a notary, a lawyer's office or a Casa Pronta desk. Then you get the keys.

You do not need to be in Portugal: many of my clients sign through a lawyer with power of attorney and only travel once the home is theirs.

Buying costs at a glance

Typical extra costs on top of the price. These are general figures: rates change and each case must be checked with your lawyer.

IMT · property transfer tax

Up to 7.5% of the price. It depends on the price and on whether it will be your permanent home.

Stamp duty (imposto do selo)

0.8% of the price, plus stamp duty on the mortgage if you take one.

Notary and land registry

Usually around €1,000 to €2,000.

Lawyer

Often around 1 to 2% of the price. Optional, but strongly recommended when you buy from abroad.

Mortgage costs

Bank fees and the bank's valuation of the home, if you finance the purchase.

5 mistakes foreigners make, and how to avoid them

  1. Skipping the lawyer. Buying from abroad, in another language, without someone checking the papers is the biggest risk you can take.
  2. Not checking the papers. Caderneta predial, land registry, energy certificate and habitation licence, before you sign anything.
  3. Forgetting the 7 to 8% in extra costs. Plan for it from day one, especially if you need a mortgage.
  4. Only visiting once. Go back at a different time of day, or ask for a second video visit. Check the noise, the light and the neighbourhood.
  5. Leaving the NIF and bank account for later. Sort them out early, so you can move fast when you find the right home.

Common questions

Can foreigners buy property in Portugal?

Yes. There are no restrictions on foreigners buying property in Portugal, whether you live in the EU or not.

Do I need to be in Portugal to buy?

No. With a power of attorney, a lawyer can sign the CPCV and the deed for you. Visits can be done on live video.

How long does it take?

Without a mortgage, it is often a matter of weeks from offer to deed. With a mortgage, plan for two to three months.

Does buying a home give me a residence permit?

Not by itself. The Golden Visa no longer accepts residential property as an investment. Visas such as the D7 (passive income) or the digital nomad visa can be combined with buying a home. An immigration lawyer can advise on your case.

What do I pay every year after buying?

The municipal property tax (IMI), usually between 0.3% and 0.45% of the property's tax value in cities, plus the building's condominium fees if it is an apartment.
