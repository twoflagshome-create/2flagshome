Buying costs in Portugal: what you pay on top of the price

The asking price is never the final price. When you buy a home in Portugal, plan for about 7 to 8% on top for taxes and fees, and a little more if you take a mortgage. Here is what each cost is, roughly how much it is, and when you pay it.

By Liliana Pinto, licensed real estate consultant in Porto (Douro Lovers, AMI 18696). I work with international clients in English, French and Portuguese.

The short answer

For a home bought without a mortgage, plan for around 7 to 8% of the price on top. Most of it is IMT and stamp duty, paid before the deed. These are general figures: rates change every year and each case must be checked with your lawyer.

IMT · property transfer tax

From 0 to 7.5% of the price, depending on the price and on whether it will be your permanent home.

Stamp duty (imposto do selo)

0.8% of the price.

Notary and land registry

Usually around €1,000 to €2,000.

Lawyer

Often around 1 to 2% of the price.

Mortgage costs

Stamp duty on the loan, bank valuation and fees, if you finance.

IMT, the property transfer tax

IMT (Imposto Municipal sobre as Transmissões Onerosas de Imóveis) is the largest cost. It is paid by the buyer before the deed, and the deed cannot be signed without proof of payment.

  • It is progressive: the rate goes up with the price, up to 7.5% for the most expensive homes.
  • Your permanent home pays less than a holiday home or an investment. If the home will not be your permanent residence, the reduced brackets do not apply.
  • It is calculated on the higher of the purchase price and the property's tax value (VPT).
  • Young buyers under 35 who are tax residents in Portugal may be exempt on their first permanent home, within a price limit.

The brackets are updated every year in the State Budget. Your lawyer or I can simulate the exact amount for a specific home; the Portuguese tax portal (Portal das Finanças) also has a simulator.

Stamp duty (imposto do selo)

Stamp duty on a purchase is 0.8% of the price (or of the tax value, if higher). It is paid together with the IMT, before the deed. If you take a mortgage, there is a separate stamp duty on the loan (see mortgage costs).

Notary and land registry

These cover signing the deed (escritura) and registering the home in your name at the land registry. Through a Casa Pronta desk the fees are fixed and lower; with a private notary or a lawyer they vary. As a guide, budget around €1,000 to €2,000 in total, a little more when a mortgage also has to be registered.

Lawyer

A lawyer is not legally required, but when you buy from abroad it is the best money you will spend. They check the papers, review the promissory contract (CPCV), handle the NIF and taxes and can sign for you with a power of attorney. Fees are often around 1 to 2% of the price, sometimes a fixed fee. Always ask for a written quote.

Mortgage costs

If you finance part of the purchase, add:

  • Stamp duty on the loan: 0.6% of the amount borrowed for loans of five years or more.
  • Stamp duty on interest: 4% of each interest payment, included in your monthly instalment.
  • Bank fees: the bank's valuation of the home (usually a few hundred euros) and the processing fee.
  • Insurance: banks require life insurance and home insurance for the length of the loan.

Costs after you buy

  • IMI, the municipal property tax: between 0.3% and 0.45% of the tax value (VPT) each year for urban homes, depending on the municipality.
  • AIMI, an additional tax only on high property values (above €600,000 of total tax value per person).
  • Condominium fees for apartments, which vary with the building and its services.
  • Home insurance, and fire insurance, which is mandatory in apartment buildings.
  • Water, electricity, gas and internet.

How to keep costs down

  1. Ask for an estimate before you make an offer. I prepare one for every home my clients are serious about.
  2. Check whether the home can be your permanent residence. It can lower the IMT considerably.
  3. Get several quotes for the lawyer and, if you need one, for the mortgage. A credit intermediary compares banks for you.
  4. Keep a margin of a few thousand euros for small surprises: certificates, translations, travel.

Common questions

Who pays the estate agent's fee?

In Portugal the agency fee is usually paid by the seller. I will always tell you upfront if a service has a cost for you.

Are costs different for foreigners?

No. Taxes and fees are the same for Portuguese and foreign buyers. What changes the IMT is whether the home is your permanent residence, not your nationality.

When do I pay the taxes?

IMT and stamp duty are paid shortly before the deed. Notary and registry fees are paid on the day of the deed. The first IMI bill arrives the following year.

Can I include the costs in my mortgage?

Usually not. Banks lend a share of the home's value, so plan to pay the taxes and fees from your own savings.
